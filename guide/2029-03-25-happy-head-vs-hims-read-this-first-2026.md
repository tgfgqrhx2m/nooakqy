Hair loss is incredibly prevalent. More than half of all men will experience it before turning 50.1 More than 50% of women will experience it at some point in their lives.2 For most of recorded history, there wasn’t much a man or woman could do to offset hair loss beyond toupees and hats.

In more recent years, scientific advancements have led to new drugs that can stop hair loss and even regrow lost hair, and numerous telemedicine companies have cropped up to offer access to those treatments online.

Happy Head and Hims are two of the top companies that offer oral and topical medications, alongside supplements and hair care products, to fight hair loss in men and women. But which company offers the better solution for you? Our team has spent years evaluating both, along with many of their competitors. We’ll delve into detailed comparisons throughout this page, pitting these two against one another directly.

For those in a hurry, our overall conclusion is that most people at this time are better off with Hims.

In every important category, Hims is the clear choice for most men, most notably when it comes to price.

Many Hims products have analogs in Happy Head’s catalog, but Happy Head charges much more. Hims’ more robust telemedicine platform is a little easier to use, too, and it offers access to a wealth of support, including numerous medications that can offset the potential side effects of hair loss treatment. Happy Head has its place, especially for younger women experiencing hair loss or men needing stronger topical treatments, but we recommend most men start with Hims. While you can find good deals on Hims’ OTC hair care products via Amazon, you’ll have to visit Hims directly to access its most effective hair loss treatments.

What are Happy Head and Hims?

Happy Head and Hims are telemedicine providers offering various solutions for hair loss. Happy Head maintains a primary focus on hair loss, though it’s recently added a single ED medication to its lineup. Conversely, Hims offers medications, products, or services for numerous issues, including hair loss, ED, skin care, and mental health.

Both companies circumvent traditional doctor's office visits and allow you to obtain prescriptions for hair loss medications like finasteride without leaving home. They also ship medications directly to your home, making for a pretty effortless experience.

Hims has about six years on Happy Head, having started in 2016 compared to Happy Head’s founding in 2019.

Hims vs. Happy Head for topical hair loss treatments

Both Hims and Happy Head offer topical hair loss treatments, but Hims offers them in more formats, including serums, sprays, and foam. Happy Head almost exclusively offers serums, with the exception of a single gel product. That gel and one serum containing an aqueous solution are the only two topicals from Happy Head that don’t contain propylene glycol, a known irritant that’s been suspected of causing several of the more common adverse reactions people have to topical hair loss treatments.25 Hims’ serums and sprays also contain it, but its minoxidil foam does not.

There are some other key differences in formulation, however, so let’s look at the two most prominent forms of topical hair loss treatment and compare them between the two companies.

Topical minoxidil from Hims and Happy Head

Minoxidil is the most common topical hair loss treatment. As the generic form of Rogaine — the same active ingredient, with about the same efficacy4 — it’s available for less than its branded counterpart. Minoxidil’s precise mechanisms of action aren’t fully understood, but studies have supported multiple theories for how it functions in a complex manner to stop hair loss and restore growth.5 One theory posits that topical minoxidil increases blood circulation at the scalp, allowing more nutrients and oxygen to reach the hair follicles.26 Another theory looks at how minoxidil may affect genetic signaling pathways, some of which are connected to stem cell development.6 In these instances, stem cells can be directed to become hair strands when they otherwise would have become something else.

Hims offers topical minoxidil in more forms than Happy Head, delivering it in a serum, a foam, and a spray. Happy Head only offers the serum and a liposomal gel. But one important area in which Happy Head’s minoxidil differs from Hims is in its 8% concentration compared to the 5-7% you get from Hims, depending on which product you get. Whether that added concentration will help of hurt your cause is going to depend largely on the individual, as stronger minoxidil concentrations don’t always provide better results.

As Dr. Bukoski puts it, “[I’m] not sure that you could call this a true superiority, as there have not been direct clinical trials to compare this exact dosage. In fact, as the dose gets higher, there may be a higher risk of irritation, which could increase shedding and plateau results.”

Topical finasteride from Happy Head and Hims

While topical minoxidil has been in use for decades, topical finasteride has only more recently become a viable alternative to oral finasteride. Studies indicate that may be about as effective as the oral version at reducing scalp dihydrotestosterone (DHT, a primary driver of pattern hair loss), but its application has a nearly undetectable effect on plasma DHT.8 9 That’s a sign that it goes to work on the scalp without getting into the bloodstream, ultimately mitigating its side effect profile. So, for men who need finasteride but are concerned with its side effects, this is a good way to go.

Commercially available topical finasteride is almost always compounded with topical minoxidil, as well, so you end up getting the positive effects of both drugs in a single application. While delivery methods and added minoxidil concentrations vary between companies, both Happy Head and Hims offer a 3% concentration of finasteride in the formulations

The biggest difference is in the delivery mechanism. Happy Head offers its topical finasteride as a serum in a bottle with a dropper cap or a gel in a pump bottle, whereas Hims provides a compounded spray or a serum. Our testers universally preferred Hims’ spray application, as it was easier to handle and far less messy.

Oral finasteride: Happy Head vs. Hims

Finasteride, the generic form of brand-name Propecia, is typically delivered as an oral medication, often at a dose of 1mg when battling hair loss.10 It’s a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor, meaning that it prevents a certain enzyme from converting testosterone into DHT. That prevents DHT from doing what it does best — causing hair loss.

Finasteride has something of a rough history, thanks in large part to a 2%-3% incidence of sexual side effects in men.11 In our conversations with long-time finasteride users who’ve experienced these side effects, we’ve learned how debilitating they can be. And additional research indicates they may last for years, even after you stop taking finasteride.30 That said, you have a 97% chance of not experiencing those side effects, so the drug may be worth a try if you’re an otherwise good candidate.

Both Hims and Happy Head offer 1mg oral finasteride tablets. Hims also offers a chewable 1.1mg finasteride tablet that’s combined with 3mg of oral minoxidil and a supplement blend.

Happy Head vs. Hims for oral minoxidil

Minoxidil was originally created as an oral medication; it was intended and is still used to treat hypertension.12 However, hypertrichosis, or excessive hair growth on the body, was a common side effect, which led to its development as a hair loss intervention. It's not a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor like finasteride, and its mechanisms of action are as mysterious orally as they are topically.

Oral minoxidil has been shown to be at least as effective as oral finasteride, but its side effect profile is meaningfully different.13 Whereas oral finasteride has sexual and depressive side effects, minoxidil’s profile is more related to the cardiovascular system.14 And since its mechanism of action isn’t related to the endocrine system, oral minoxidil is offered by commercial companies for both men and women (as opposed to finasteride/dutasteride for females).

Some research has indicated that people with healthy blood pressure haven’t seen blood pressure effects from larger doses of oral minoxidil than those used to fight hair loss, but it’s still a risk you should discuss with your doctor.15

While both Hims and Happy Head offer oral minoxidil, Hims’ doses are a bit higher, and its minoxidil comes in a convenient chewable format. At that higher dose, Hims actually presents a better deal, especially if you opt for quarterly or 5-month billing. You can also get a compounded chewable version of minoxidil that’s combined with 1.1mg of finasteride for the same price.

Hims didn’t run away with this category, however. Happy Head also offers its minoxidil as a standalone or combined with other oral treatments to potentially increase efficacy. These combinations make Happy Head more attractive for both male and female customers looking to combine therapies, as some of them include additional prescription ingredients applicable to one sex or the other (e.g., finasteride for men or spironolactone for women).

Happy Head also offers oral minoxidil in several of its Super Capsules, where it’s combined with various other ingredients like finasteride, dutasteride, spironolactone, and vitamin D. Male customers may want to steer clear of the Super Capsules, however, only because their 1.25mg minoxidil dose is on the low end of supported clinical data.31

Female customers may save more with Happy Head if they can find success at the relatively low 1.25mg dose in the standalone tablet. At that dose, a customer could pay as little as $22/month for their medication, and some research suggests that a dose of around 1mg can be effective at stopping hair loss in nearly 80% of women, while regrowing hair in around 15%.16 The Super Capsule with 1.25mg minoxidil and 25mg spironolactone may increase the efficacy.

Supportive hair products: Happy Head vs. Hims

When doing all you can to fight hair loss, you may not want to stop at one or two medicinal interventions. Shampoos, conditioners, and nutritional supplements may be able to work in conjunction with medicinal interventions to support your overall hair health. Evidence for regrowth with most supplemental treatments is incredibly sparse, and none of these would likely work on their own. Some ingredients may increase the apparent thickness of your hair or create more favorable conditions for things like topical treatments to do their work, but they likely won’t drive growth themselves.

“Hair growth supplementation, including shampoos and conditioners, does not have substantial supportive clinical evidence as the previously discussed medications do,” Dr. Bukoski says. “People shouldn't go spending all of their money trying to supplement their way to hair growth.”

Both Hims and Happy Head offer these products and more, though there are some noteworthy differences between the two companies. Let’s take a look at each category of supportive hair care products from Hims and Happy Head.

Our biggest issues with Happy Head are its cancellation process and billing cycle. If you want to cancel a subscription, you have to call customer service. In this day and age, we find this decidedly unnecessary, and it raises the cynical thought that it’s designed to prevent users from canceling with ease.

The other issue with a Happy Head subscription is the billing cycle. Unlike Hims, which offers multiple billing cycles in 30-day increments (though not all cycles are available for all products), Happy Head only offers a 25-day billing cycle.

In theory, the 25-day billing cycle ensures you never run out of a daily-use product. But in practice, it causes a constantly fluctuating billing and shipping date, complicating household budgets. It also raises the likelihood that you'll forget to end a subscription you intended to cancel until it's too late to stop a shipment.

Hims doesn’t suffer from either of these issues, but the company lags behind Happy Head in price transparency. Through Happy Head, you need to message the site’s chatbot to receive a link to your messaging platform, which is hosted by a third-party site. Alternatives to Happy Head and Hims

If a head-to-head comparison between these two companies hasn’t convinced you of one’s superiority over the other, you may be interested in products or services from one of their competitors. Some of these are direct competitors that offer various forms of finasteride or minoxidil, while others provide different treatments altogether.

Keeps

Keeps is one of the most prominent names in the hair loss space. It spent years with a sole focus on hair loss, offering minoxidil, finasteride, and, at one point, hair restoration surgery. Today, the company has expanded to include ED medications and other sexual wellness products, and it offers one thing its competitors do not: a thickening pomade.

Roman

Roman more closely resembles Hims than it does Happy Head, with a bevy of treatments for things like ED, premature ejaculation, weight loss, and photoaging. Many of its prices are higher compared to competitors, but its prices for oral hair loss medications are among the best in the business.

Laser caps

Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) is a form of photobiomodulation that’s about as effective as topical minoxidil for treating androgenetic alopecia. Sessions involve wearing a small ball cap or helmet outfitted with around 200-300 laser diodes tuned to a specific frequency that studies show can stop hair loss and stimulate new growth.24 Laser caps are more expensive than a few months' worth of minoxidil, but they’re far less messy, and you only have to pay for them once.
