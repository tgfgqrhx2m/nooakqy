Internal Medicine

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is a common inner ear disorder that causes brief episodes of dizziness or spinning sensations triggered by specific head movements, such as rolling over in bed, tilting your head back, or bending down. It occurs when tiny calcium carbonate crystals (otoconia) become dislodged from their normal location in the inner ear and shift into the semicircular canals, disrupting your body's balance signals to the brain. Episodes typically last less than a minute but can be intense and disorienting.

Why Does This Cause Dizziness?

The inner ear has tiny canals filled with fluid that help you keep your balance.

When these particles move into the canals, they can disrupt the fluid's movement.

This sends confusing signals to your brain about your body's position, causing dizziness.
