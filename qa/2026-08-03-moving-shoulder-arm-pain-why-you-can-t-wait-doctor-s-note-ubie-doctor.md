Internal Medicine

Moving pain in the shoulder and arm is often caused by nerve-related conditions such as cervical radiculopathy (a pinched nerve in the neck) or Parsonage-Turner syndrome (sudden inflammation of shoulder nerves). Both conditions affect the nerves that travel from the neck through the shoulder and down the arm, which is why pain can shift or radiate between these areas. Other possible causes include rotator cuff injuries, thoracic outlet syndrome, or referred pain from the spine.

Because shifting shoulder and arm pain can stem from many overlapping conditions, identifying the true source early is key to getting the right treatment and avoiding long-term nerve damage. Explanation

Moving pain in the shoulder and arm can happen for a couple of reasons. One common cause is cervical radiculopathy, which occurs when nerves in the neck get pinched or irritated. This can lead to pain that moves down the arm, and sometimes, a special position called the shoulder abduction relief sign can help ease the pain.

Another possible cause is Parsonage-Turner syndrome. This is a condition where a bundle of nerves near the shoulder called the brachial plexus become inflamed, leading to sudden and severe pain. The pain can spread and move around the shoulder and arm. Over time, this can also cause weakness in the shoulder and arm muscles.
