Internal Medicine

Anxiety medications are generally not effective for treating sleepwalking and may actually worsen episodes in some cases. Certain sedatives and anti-anxiety drugs can disrupt normal sleep cycles, potentially increasing the frequency or intensity of sleepwalking behaviors. If you or a loved one experiences sleepwalking, it's important to consult a doctor before starting, stopping, or changing any medication.

Because sleepwalking can be linked to a wide range of underlying causes—including stress, sleep deprivation, medications, or other sleep disorders—understanding what's driving your symptoms is the crucial first step. Explanation

Sleepwalking is a type of parasomnia, which is a disorder that involves unwanted events or experiences while sleeping. Some medications for anxiety, like antidepressants, might not reduce sleepwalking and can sometimes make it worse.

Medications and Sleepwalking: According to the studies, anxiety medications, particularly certain antidepressants, might not help with sleepwalking. In some cases, they could even trigger or worsen sleepwalking behaviors.

Consult a Doctor: If you are experiencing sleepwalking and are on anxiety medication, it's important to consult with a healthcare provider. They can help determine the best course of action and adjust medications if needed.
