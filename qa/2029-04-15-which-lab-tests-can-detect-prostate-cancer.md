PSA is a protein generated by the prostate gland, a tiny organ found under the bladder in males. PSA’s major purpose is to liquefy semen, however a limited quantity of this protein reaches the circulation.2

The PSA test involves taking a blood sample from the patient and sending it to a laboratory for examination. The test determines the levels of PSA in the blood, which is commonly expressed as nanograms per milliliter of blood (ng/mL).

The pace at which PSA levels climb over time, known as PSA velocity, might potentially be a crucial consideration.3 A quick increase in PSA values may indicate an increased risk of prostate cancer.

What does a routine blood test cover?

A routine blood test is used to check for a range of things, including your blood count and the levels of certain chemicals and substances in your blood. Blood tests can also be used to check how well certain organs, such as your liver and kidneys, are functioning.

How is a blood sample collected for lab testing?

A blood sample for lab testing is typically collected through a process called venipuncture, which involves inserting a small needle into a vein to draw blood. This is usually done on the inside of the elbow or the back of the hand.
