Internal Medicine

Shingles on the face or around the eye typically appears as a painful, blistering rash on one side of the face. Common symptoms include burning pain, itching, redness, and fluid-filled blisters that may crust over. When shingles affects the eye area (ophthalmic shingles), it can also cause eye redness, swelling, light sensitivity, and blurred vision. Early treatment is critical to prevent complications like vision loss or long-term nerve pain.

Explanation

Shingles, also known as herpes zoster, is a condition that can cause a rash with blisters on the skin. When it affects the face or area around the eye, it usually appears as a red, painful rash with blisters that can be very itchy.

Location: The rash often appears on one side of the face or around one eye.

Symptoms: It can be very painful and itchy, and some people may feel a tingling or burning sensation before the rash appears.

Shingles happens because of the same virus that causes chickenpox. After someone has chickenpox, the virus can stay in their body and come back later as shingles. The pain and itchiness from shingles can be severe, and sometimes people may continue to feel these symptoms even after the rash goes away. This ongoing pain or itch is known as postherpetic itch.
