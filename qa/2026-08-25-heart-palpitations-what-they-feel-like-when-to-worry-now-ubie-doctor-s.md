Internal Medicine

Heart palpitations are the sensation that your heart is beating too hard, too fast, skipping beats, or fluttering in your chest. You may feel them in your chest, throat, or neck, and they can occur during activity or at rest. While palpitations are often harmless and triggered by stress, caffeine, exercise, or hormonal changes, they can sometimes signal an underlying condition such as arrhythmia, thyroid issues, anemia, or heart disease.

Because the causes of heart palpitations range from mild to serious, understanding what's driving your symptoms is the critical first step. Explanation

People often describe heart palpitations as a thumping or pounding sensation in the chest as though the heart were beating harder or faster than usual. While many people think palpitations originate from the heart beating faster or harder, it is a perception or feeling. Palpitations may be from a slower heart beat, skipped heart beats, extra heart beats or not be coming from your heart at all.
