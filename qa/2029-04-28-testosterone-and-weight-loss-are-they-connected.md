Does Testosterone Reduce Belly Fat?

Mechanism of Testosterone in Reducing Abdominal Fat

Increased fat production can be caused by a myriad of factors. For instance, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and diabetes are all associated with increased fat production. Moreover, low testosterone bioavailability is linked to cardiovascular diseases, decreased insulin sensitivity and metabolic syndrome. So it shouldn’t be surprising that testosterone is considered an inhibitor of fat production.

Maybe you’re wondering how exactly testosterone contributes to the reduction of fat mass. Lipogenesis, or the production of fat, happens when liver and fat cells use carbs and proteins and convert them into lipids (fat). Testosterone is known as a fat-reducing hormone due to its prevention of lipid uptake by fat cells and stimulation of lipid breakdown. While more recent research suggests that the exact mechanisms may very well be tissue and species dependent, it has been clear for some time that testosterone blocks fat production.

Comparative Studies and Results

Earlier research around testosterone’s fat inhibition suggested that testosterone stops fat cell maturity. Along with this research were explanations proposing that testosterone reduces fat cell’s size possibly in conjunction with inhibiting maturity.

The consensus of recent studies include two major mechanisms by which testosterone may inhibit lipid production. Adipocytes are cells purposed to store fat and have receptors along them that can regulate their biochemical behavior. Research has suggested both androgen receptors and estrogen receptors in adipocytes are activated through testosterone and aromatized testosterone respectively. This means bioavailable testosterone or testosterone aromatized into estrogen binds and reduces lipogenesis through fat cell mediation.

Does Testosterone Speed Up Metabolism?

Understanding Metabolism and Its Role in Weight Management

To better understand the fundamentals of how our metabolism impacts our weight and its management requires us to discuss what our metabolism is and how it works. Our metabolism refers to the many different biological and chemical reactions that take place to break down and build up the molecules of our body. These reactions and processes include protein production, carbohydrate breakdown, and cell repair, all of which rely on our metabolism to regulate the conversion of food and oxygen into energy.

Our metabolism and its processes are constant; this means your body has a base rate of converting calories into energy. This rate is also known as your basal metabolic rate, and it is the amount of calories your body needs to maintain all functions at complete rest. When a metabolism is not impacted by chronic illnesses or disorders, your basal metabolic rate can help you create a weight management plan.

In order to maintain your weight, you have to eat the same amount of calories burned by your basal metabolic rate and any additional daily physical activity. Losing or gaining weight would require eating less or more calories than your basal metabolic rate and any additional daily physical activity expended.

Testosterone’s Effect on Metabolic Rate

Prior research suggested there is not a direct link between testosterone and basal metabolic rate in individuals AMAB. However, there is a wealth of modern research showing testosterone’s effects on factors that impact our metabolism.

For example, testosterone has been shown to increase muscle mass, and muscle requires more calories to maintain than fat, which ‘speeds up’ a metabolism. Moreover, studies highlight testosterone as a key player with mechanisms that stimulate carbohydrate, lipid and protein metabolism. While the pathways are still being uncovered, restoring testosterone is also seen to improve the body’s sensitivity to insulin and as a result reduce blood sugar levels. All of these metabolic processes have been linked to testosterone which shows testosterone’s indirect effect on our metabolism.
