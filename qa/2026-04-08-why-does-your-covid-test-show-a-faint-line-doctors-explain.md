# Why Does Your Covid Test Show a Faint Line? Doctors Explain.

MOST COVID TESTING is done at home these days. That means it’s up to you to swab your nose properly and interpret your test results accurately.

In some instances, that can lead to confusion and lots of questions. One that tends to come up relates to the lightness or darkness of the lines that appear on your rapid antigen test—and, whether you actually have Covid if your test shows a faint line.

When it comes to dark versus faint lines, “both should be interpreted that you’ve got Covid,” explains Thomas Russo, M.D., an infectious disease expert and professor at the University at Buffalo. “It’s really that simple.”

Covid tests are different, but for most tests, one line means it’s negative. Two lines, no matter how faint they are, mean the test is positive.

But there are a few reasons the line on your test may appear lighter. Dr. Russo says it could be related to your viral load or how well you swabbed your nostril to collect the sample.

“If that faint line pops up, you should take it seriously,” he adds. That means isolating yourself to avoid infecting others and talking to your doctor about treatments like Paxlovid if you’re not feeling well.

Testing for Covid remains an essential step in keeping the virus from spreading. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommends self-testing when you have Covid symptoms, such as fever, chills, cough, fatigue, and body aches. It's also recommended at least five days after being exposed to someone with Covid.

While it does mean you have Covid, there are a few things to know about a faint line on a Covid test.

At-home Covid tests work by detecting viral proteins, known as Covid antigens, says Carlo Manzana, M.D., a family medicine physician at the telemedicine platform PlushCare.

“When the antigen is present in the sample, it triggers the appearance of a line next to the words ‘sample’ or ‘test’ on the test strip,” he says. “This typically indicates a positive result.”

The line might appear lighter if you don’t have a high concentration of the Covid antigen when you test, Dr. Manzana says.

Another issue that might affect how light or dark the line appears on your Covid test is how well you swab the inside of your nose, Dr. Russo says.

“Some people do a very superficial job; some people do a more vigorous job,” he explains. “That could result in different amounts of virus in the swab and then slight differences in reactivity of the test.”

What can a faint line tell you about your viral load?

During the natural course of a Covid infection, you shed more virus at different times, Dr. Russo says. So, when you test might affect the shade of the line appearing on your test. A faint line could suggest that your viral load (or how much of the virus is in your body) is low.

While there isn’t research on the viral load of the current Covid variants, a 2023 study published in Clinical Infectious Diseases found that viral loads of the Omicron variant peaked on the fourth day after symptoms started. However, early in the pandemic, viral loads were highest at the onset of symptoms and then decreased.

“If you test on day one or two with symptoms, if your viral load is less, that band may be a little fainter than if you test a day or two later,” Dr. Russo says. “That’s because the viral load has increased over those days.”

Research has shown that rapid antigen tests are sensitive enough to detect someone’s viral load even when they don’t have symptoms. When someone takes a test, a darker line or a line that appears quickly means a higher viral load, or that someone is at peak infectiousness. A lighter line that takes up to 15 minutes to appear means a low viral load.

At-home Covid test are said to be about 80 percent accurate. But, it depends on how well someone collects their sample. Swabbing inside your nose can be unpleasant, and if you don’t follow the directions on the test, which usually say to swab both nostrils five times for about 15 sections, it could affect the test result.

“A faint line could mean that someone’s viral load is not as high as if the line were darker, but it could also be caused by not getting an adequate sample while swabbing,” Dr. Manzana says.

How contagious are you if your test shows a faint line?

Even if your positive Covid test shows a faint line, you’re still likely shedding virus and could be contagious to others, Dr. Russo says. However, if your viral load is low, you might not be as contagious.

How to Test Yourself Correctly

Since at-home Covid tests differ, reading and following the instructions on your specific test is crucial for getting an accurate result, Dr. Manzana says.

The biggest problem people run into when testing at home is using the nasal swab, Dr. Russo says. “You don't want to be doing the old brain biopsy and really ramming it up there. You want to stick to the anterior part of your nose.”

Check your individual test kit, but most say to swab the inside of each nostril for about 15 seconds or about five circular motions.
