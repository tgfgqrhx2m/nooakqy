Symptoms of the stomach flu include:

Diarrhea that is not bloody

Abdominal cramps and pain

Nausea

Vomiting

Occasional muscle aches

Headaches

Low-grade fever

Depending on the cause of the virus, you may begin experiencing symptoms within one to three days after you have been infected. Symptoms range from mild to severe. Symptoms usually only last for a day or two, but they may persist for up to 10 days.

When to see a doctor

The stomach flu typically resolves on its own within a few days, depending on what type of bacteria is ailing you. Be sure to call your doctor if you are not able to keep liquids down for a whole day, you’ve been vomiting for more than two days, you’re vomiting blood, you’re dehydrated, there is blood in your stool, or if you have a fever above 104 degrees Fahrenheit.

Diagnosis and treatment

Your doctor can diagnose gastroenteritis based on symptoms, a physical exam, and sometimes based on the presence of similar cases in your community. They may also want you to do a stool sample.

There is usually no specific treatment for the stomach flu other than dietary changes, medications to improve your symptoms, and time. Effective nutritional changes include drinking lots of fluids to combat dehydration, not eating solid food, and adhering to the BRAT diet (bananas, rice, apple sauce, and toast) when you do eat solid foods. You should also make sure you get plenty of rest, as the virus can cause you to feel weak and tired.
