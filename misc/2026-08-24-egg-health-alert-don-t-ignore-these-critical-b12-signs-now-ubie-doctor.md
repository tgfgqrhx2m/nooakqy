The Energy Vitamin and Your Eggs: Signs of Deficiency and Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Vitamin B12 deficiency can impair fertility by disrupting DNA synthesis, raising homocysteine levels, and interfering with ovulation. These effects may reduce egg quality, hinder embryo implantation, and increase miscarriage risk in both natural conception and assisted reproduction.

Key considerations include identifying who is at higher risk (vegans, vegetarians, older adults, and those with absorption issues), recognizing symptoms like fatigue, numbness, and brain fog, confirming deficiency through blood tests, meeting recommended daily intakes, exploring dietary sources, oral supplements, or B12 injections, and checking related nutrients such as folate and iron. Explanation

When you're trying to support your fertility, nutrition matters. One nutrient that doesn't always get enough attention is vitamin B12, often called the "energy vitamin." It plays a critical role in nerve health, red blood cell production, and DNA synthesis. But many people don't realize it also plays a role in reproductive health.

So, can a lack of Vitamin B12 cause infertility? The short answer: it can contribute to fertility problems, especially if the deficiency is significant and untreated. Let's break down how B12 affects your body, your eggs, and what to do if you suspect a deficiency.

What Is Vitamin B12 and Why Does It Matter?

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is a water-soluble vitamin found primarily in animal products such as:

Eggs

It is essential for:

Making healthy red blood cells

Supporting the nervous system

Building and repairing DNA

Helping cells divide properly

Because egg cells and early embryos rely heavily on healthy DNA replication and cell division, B12 plays an indirect but important role in fertility.

Can a Lack of Vitamin B12 Cause Infertility?

Yes, a lack of Vitamin B12 can contribute to infertility, especially when the deficiency is moderate to severe.

Here's how:

1. Impaired DNA Synthesis

Vitamin B12 is essential for DNA production. Without adequate levels:

Cells cannot divide properly

Egg quality may decline

Embo development may be impaired

This may reduce the chances of successful fertilization or implantation.

2. Elevated Homocysteine Levels

Low B12 can increase homocysteine, an amino acid in the blood. High homocysteine levels have been linked to:

Ovulation problems

Poor egg quality

Increased risk of miscarriage

Implantation failure

Balanced B12 levels help keep homocysteine under control.

3. Ovulatory Dysfunction

Severe B12 deficiency may interfere with:

Regular menstrual cycles

Proper ovulation

While it is rarely the sole cause of infertility, it can be one contributing factor—especially when combined with other nutritional deficiencies.

4. Increased Risk During Early Pregnancy

Even if conception occurs, low B12 levels may increase the risk of:

Early pregnancy loss

Neural tube defects in the developing baby

Anemia during pregnancy

For these reasons, adequate B12 is important before and during pregnancy.

Who Is at Higher Risk for B12 Deficiency?

Not everyone is equally at risk. You may be more vulnerable if you:

Follow a vegan or strict vegetarian diet

Have had weight-loss or gastric surgery

Have digestive disorders (Crohn's disease, celiac disease)

Take metformin or long-term acid-reducing medications

Are over age 50

Have pernicious anemia

Because B12 absorption requires stomach acid and intrinsic factor (a protein made in the stomach), digestive issues can significantly reduce absorption—even if your diet contains enough B12.

How Is B12 Deficiency Diagnosed?

A doctor can evaluate B12 status with:

Serum vitamin B12 blood test

Methylmalonic acid (MMA) test

Homocysteine levels

Complete blood count (CBC)

Sometimes, "normal" lab values can still mask functional deficiency. If fertility is a concern, it's reasonable to ask for a thorough evaluation.

How Much Vitamin B12 Do You Need?

For most adults, the recommended daily amount is:

2.4 micrograms per day

During pregnancy:

2.6 micrograms per day

However, individuals with deficiency may need much higher doses temporarily under medical supervision.

Can Correcting B12 Improve Fertility?

In individuals with documented deficiency, correcting low B12 levels may:

Improve ovulation

Lower homocysteine levels

Support egg quality

Reduce miscarriage risk

However, infertility is often multifactorial. B12 is rarely the only cause, but it can be one piece of the puzzle.

The Bottom Line

So, can a lack of Vitamin B12 cause infertility?

Yes, it can contribute—particularly by impairing DNA synthesis, raising homocysteine levels, and affecting ovulation and early embryo development. Severe or prolonged deficiency may increase miscarriage risk and pregnancy complications.

The good news: B12 deficiency is usually treatable once identified.
