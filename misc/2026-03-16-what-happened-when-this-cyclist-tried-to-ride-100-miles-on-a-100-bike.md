Welsh cyclist Manon Lloyd had won multiple medals in international competitions by the time she was 20. But how far and fast can a star athlete go without their usual elite kit? In her latest challenge video as a host on the Global Cycling Network's YouTube channel, Lloyd was given a budget of just £100 (around $130) and tasked with traveling 100 miles.

When the standard eBay search failed her, Lloyd ended up paying a visit to a man who was selling bikes out of his front yard, and went home with her newly purchased secondhand Triumph Tempest. After giving it the once-over, she switched out the saddle and pedals for her own, making it a more comfortable ride—even if it does "weigh a ton."

Lloyd wakes up bright and early for a 5.30 a.m. start to her ride around the Peak District, which includes 7,000 feet of climbing. "This would be a challenge for me on a super-bike, let alone my cheap bike," she says. "I'm not going to lie, I'm a little bit worried about descending on this bad boy, as the brakes aren't the best."

Her first impressions of the bike end up being better than she expected; it's a little heavy on inclines, but the gears are quite smooth, and the brakes are fine as long as she starts braking earlier than usual. But it's nowhere near as responsive as the bike Lloyd is used to riding, which becomes more and more frustrating as the journey continues.

As the hills on the ride get steeper, the bike starts to handle worse, and Lloyd has to push even harder to keep going. Then, at 8 a.m., 60 km into the ride, the front tire blows, and Lloyd has to track down a bike shop in a nearby village in order to get it repaired.

Once she has resumed her journey, the fatigue from riding such a heavy bike really starts to take its toll on Lloyd's legs. "On the climbs it's just horrible, it's a proper slog, it just has not been built for climbing with the gears, and it's so heavy," she says. "I don't even like the downhills, I get scared of the brakes, I don't know what's worse, uphill or downhill."
