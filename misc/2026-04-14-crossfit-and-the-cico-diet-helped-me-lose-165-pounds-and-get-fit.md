# CrossFit and the CICO Diet Helped Me Lose 165 Pounds and Get Fit

Drew Whitfield is 29-year-old, Florida-based web and graphic designer. Dissatisfied with his weight, he decided to do something about it, using simple techniques like the CICO diet to lose more than 165 pounds. Here, in his own words, he explains how he did it.

I’ve been overweight ever since I was a child. Growing up and into adulthood, I just didn’t have a very healthy diet. On top of not being very active, I ate mostly junk food—lots of it. I didn’t eat anything nutritious, and that really took its toll.

At 27 years old, I tipped the scales at almost 350 pounds. That was my heaviest, and that kind of weight puts a strain on your body. I had almost no energy; I felt really sluggish, and was constantly tired. I had a very narrow range of motion, too.

I went through a really rough divorce. That was my lowest point, physically and emotionally. Looking at where I was at that point, I recognized that I needed to change. If I kept up the way I was going, I’d be looking at serious health problems, even an early death. That wasn’t what I wanted for myself, so I knew I had to do something about it.

I started by tackling my diet. I wanted something simple that I could do consistently, so I just focused on calories in, calories out, or CICO. Running a caloric deficit, no matter what I ate, meant that I’d be making progress every day, even if I couldn’t see it at first.

As I got my diet under control, I started going to the gym regularly. I found CrossFit, but I wasn’t doing anything very intensive—just light weights interspersed with a lot of cardio. CrossFit worked for me, but really it could have been any kind of consistent exercise; the idea was just to keep moving. I had a few trainers who helped me with my lifting technique, and helped me make my workouts as efficient as possible.

One big challenge was staying motivated. I started from a pretty rough place. Deciding to lose 100 pounds would have made the progress seem really slow, so I broke it up into smaller goals. Each milestone I hit along the way reminded me that I was moving forward, no matter how I felt from one day to the next.

I lost most of the weight in a little over a year, going from 350 pounds to 185 pounds. Losing almost half of my body weight, it’s a night-and-day comparison to how I feel now. I’ve gone from listless and sluggish to feeling like I have so much energy—my body thanks me every day.

A lot of friends and family were shocked to see me lose so much weight, but everyone was supportive. I couldn’t have done it without having them in my corner. I came out of it feeling more confident in myself; I’m much more comfortable with travel and social situations now, in a way I wasn’t before. I feel like I’ve truly started to love myself.

From here, I want to build more muscle and keep progressing with my strength training. I'm nowhere close to being done, but that's the fun of it.

For anyone looking to make a big change in their life, I’d encourage them to look at it one day at a time. Find a routine that you can do every day; consistency is key. You’re building habits not just for the short term, but for the rest of your life.
