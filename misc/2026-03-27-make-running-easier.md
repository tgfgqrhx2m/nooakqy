# Make Running Easier

[Make Running Easier](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/blog/0183058969.html)

Shorten your stride to make your next run feel easier, says a new study presented Wednesday at the American College of Sports Medicine's annual meeting. Bonus for competitive guys: The technique could also help you run faster and longer.

When runners in the study increased their leg turnover while maintaining the same speed on a treadmill, they improved their VO2 max—meaning they were able to process and use oxygen more efficiently—researchers found. What’s more, when stride frequency increased 4 percent, the subjects’ average heart rates dropped 4 percent. (Remember: Their speed didn’t change.)

The stride fix improved what scientists call running economy, which means that you can use less physical effort to run at the same speed. (Tip: Hill workouts are another great way to improve running economy.)

Another benefit: “If you shorten your stride when you race, you can increase your sub-maximal speed and maintain it for a longer period of time,” says lead study author, Timothy Quinn, F.A.C.S.M., of the University of New Hampshire. Translation? You’ll have more energy to run faster, longer, and with enough juice left over to sprint through the finish line.

Quinn speculates a short stride allows you to plant your foot in a way that generates more force, which makes it easier to propel yourself along, partly sparing your muscles.
