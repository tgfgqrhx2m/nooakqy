How to Gain Weight Gluten-Free: Important High-Calorie Choices

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Gaining weight on a gluten-free diet is possible by eating nutrient-dense, calorie-rich foods like healthy fats, quality proteins, and gluten-free carbohydrates to support steady, sustainable weight gain.

Key strategies include:

Calculating your daily calorie needs and eating in a surplus

Adding healthy fats like olive oil, avocado, or nut butters to meals

Choosing gluten-free carbs such as rice, quinoa, and potatoes

Timing protein-rich meals around workouts

Tracking progress weekly to adjust intake

However, unintended weight loss or difficulty gaining weight on a gluten-free diet can sometimes signal an underlying health issue, such as nutrient malabsorption, thyroid problems, or other conditions that need attention. If you're struggling to gain weight despite eating enough, it's important to understand why. Explanation

Gaining weight on a gluten-free diet can feel challenging. Without wheat, barley or rye, it's easy to cut calories unintentionally. Yet with the right approach, you can build a healthy, sustainable plan to add pounds—muscle and healthy fat—while staying gluten-free.

Why Healthy Weight Gain Matters

Focusing on nutritious, calorie-dense foods ensures you gain weight without compromising health. Rapid, unhealthy weight gain can lead to blood sugar spikes, digestive distress or unwanted fat accumulation. Instead, aim for gradual progress—about 0.5–1 pound per week—so your body adapts smoothly.

Calculate Your Calorie Needs

Before choosing foods, estimate how many calories you burn each day:

Basal metabolic rate (BMR): energy used at rest

Activity level: exercise, daily movement

Digestion: the calories used to process food

Use an online BMR calculator, then add 300–500 calories for gradual weight gain. Track intake for a week to fine-tune your target.

High-Calorie, Gluten-Free Foods

Healthy Fats

Avocados and guacamole

Nuts (almonds, walnuts, pecans) and nut butters (peanut, almond, cashew)

Seeds (chia, flax, pumpkin) and seed butters

Olive oil, avocado oil, coconut oil

Olives and tapenades

Protein-Rich Choices

Eggs (whole eggs are more calorie-dense than egg whites)

Full-fat dairy (Greek yogurt, cheese, cottage cheese)

Lean meats (chicken, turkey) and fattier cuts (pork chops, ribeye)

Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)

Legumes (beans, lentils) plus rice or a gluten-free grain

Gluten-free protein powders (pea, rice, whey isolate)

Calorie-Dense Carbs

Gluten-free grains: quinoa, buckwheat, millet, sorghum

Rice and rice-based products (rice noodles, rice cakes)

Potatoes (white, red, sweet) and winter squashes (butternut, acorn)

Gluten-free oats (certified gluten-free) for oatmeal or granola

Corn products: polenta, corn tortillas

Snacks & Extras

Dried fruits (dates, raisins, apricots)

Trail mix (nuts + seeds + dried fruit + dark chocolate)

Gluten-free granola bars or energy bites

Cheese and cold-cut roll-ups

Hummus with vegetable sticks or rice crackers

Strategies to Boost Calories Easily

Add extra oil or butter when cooking vegetables, grains and proteins.

Stir a spoonful of nut butter or coconut oil into smoothies, oatmeal and sauces.

Sprinkle grated cheese or shredded coconut over salads, soups and snacks.

Choose full-fat dairy over low-fat versions.

Meal Timing and Frequency

Eating more often can help you pack in calories without feeling overly full:

Three balanced meals + three snacks per day

Time a snack 30–60 minutes before exercise

Have a protein-rich snack or shake within an hour after workouts

Consider a small bedtime snack (cottage cheese, nut butter toast)

Track Progress and Adjust

Weigh yourself once a week, same time of day, same clothes

Keep a simple food diary or use a tracking app

If weight gain stalls, add 100–200 calories per day

Focus on strength training to encourage muscle gain

When to Seek Medical Advice

If you struggle to gain weight or experience symptoms like chronic fatigue, digestive distress or unintended weight loss, check your symptoms now with Ubie's free AI-powered symptom checker to help identify possible underlying causes and determine when professional care is needed.

Always speak to a doctor about any persistent or serious concerns. Underlying conditions—such as thyroid disorders, malabsorption syndromes or food intolerances—can impact weight and require professional evaluation.

Final Thoughts

Gaining weight on a gluten-free diet is entirely possible with planned, calorie-dense meals and snacks. Focus on healthy fats, quality proteins and gluten-free carbohydrate sources. Track your progress, adjust as needed, and don't hesitate to seek medical guidance for any concerning issues. Remember: gradual, steady gains are often more sustainable and healthier in the long run.
