Hormonal birth control—including combination pills, patches, and rings containing estrogen—can raise the risk of blood clots (venous thromboembolism), stroke, and heart attack, particularly in people who smoke, are over 35, have high blood pressure, or have a history of clotting disorders. Explanation

Hormonal birth control, like the pill, can affect your body in different ways. Some studies show that using these contraceptives can increase the risk of blood clots, which are clumps of blood that can block blood vessels. This is important because it can lead to serious problems like strokes or heart attacks.

Potential Health Concerns

Blood Clots: Research indicates that women using hormonal birth control may have a higher chance of developing blood clots. These clots can be dangerous if they travel to the lungs or brain.
