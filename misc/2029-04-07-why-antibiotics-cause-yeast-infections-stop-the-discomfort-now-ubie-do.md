Internal Medicine

Yes, antibiotics can cause yeast infections. Antibiotics kill both harmful and beneficial bacteria in the body. When helpful bacteria—like Lactobacillus—are eliminated, the natural microbial balance is disrupted. This allows Candida yeast to overgrow, leading to infections in areas such as the vagina, mouth, or skin.

Common symptoms include itching, burning, unusual discharge, or white patches. Risk is higher with broad-spectrum antibiotics, prolonged use, or in people with weakened immune systems or diabetes.
