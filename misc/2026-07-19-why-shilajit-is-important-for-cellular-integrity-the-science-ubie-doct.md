Composition of Shilajit

Shilajit is a complex mixture of organic and inorganic components. Key constituents include:

Fulvic acid (40–60%): A major bioactive molecule that helps transport minerals into cells and exhibits antioxidant properties.

Role in Antioxidant Defense

Oxidative stress occurs when free radicals overwhelm the body's antioxidant defenses, damaging lipids, proteins, and DNA. Shilajit helps counteract this through:

Free-radical scavenging: Fulvic and humic acids neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS).

Upregulation of endogenous antioxidants: Animal studies show increased levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase after shilajit supplementation.

Lipid peroxidation reduction: Markers of cell-membrane damage, such as malondialdehyde (MDA), decrease in response to shilajit.

By strengthening antioxidant defenses, shilajit protects cellular components from oxidative injury, a key driver of aging and disease.

Impact on Mitochondrial Function

Mitochondria are the powerhouses of cells, generating ATP through oxidative phosphorylation. Dysfunctional mitochondria produce excess ROS and fail to meet energy demands. Shilajit supports mitochondrial health by:

Enhancing ATP production: Dibenzo-alpha-pyrones facilitate electron transport, leading to more efficient energy generation.

Stabilizing mitochondrial membranes: Minerals and fulvic acid help maintain membrane integrity, preventing excessive leakage of ROS.

Promoting mitochondrial biogenesis: Animal models suggest shilajit may activate PGC-1α, a key regulator of new mitochondria formation.

Improved mitochondrial function ensures cells have adequate energy for repair processes, including those that safeguard DNA integrity.

Enhancement of DNA Repair Capacity

Maintaining Shilajit and DNA repair capacity is central to preserving cellular integrity. DNA can suffer damage from UV light, environmental toxins, by-products of metabolism, and normal replication errors. Unrepaired DNA lesions can lead to mutations, impaired cell function, or programmed cell death (apoptosis).

Research highlights include:

Upregulation of repair enzymes: Rodent studies show increased expression of DNA polymerase β and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) after shilajit treatment, both critical to base-excision repair pathways.

Reduced DNA fragmentation: In vitro studies on human cell lines reveal that shilajit reduces the number of DNA strand breaks following exposure to oxidative stressors.

Protection against UV-induced damage: Fulvic acid in shilajit attenuates the formation of cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers (CPDs), a common UV-induced lesion.

Safety and Usage

Shilajit is generally well tolerated when sourced and processed appropriately. Key considerations:

Purity matters: Raw or unprocessed shilajit may contain heavy metals. Always choose standardized, lab-tested preparations.

Typical dosing: 200–500 mg per day of purified extract, split into two doses, is commonly studied.

Possible interactions: Shilajit may enhance the effects of antioxidant medications or chelators; consult your doctor if you're on prescription therapies.

Side effects: Rare and mild, including digestive upset or dizziness, usually resolve with dose adjustment.

Before starting any supplement, especially if you have chronic conditions or are pregnant, consult a healthcare professional.
