Ramon Dino is as classic as classic gets. The classic physique competitor has a refreshing, old-school approach to bodybuilding, and invests most of his attention towards building overall strength rather than solely focusing on an aesthetic physique.

As such, he swears by compound lifts. Deadlifts, squats, and bench press are all Ramon Dino staples.

In a world where most bodybuilders are focused on crafting the perfect physique by isolating each singular muscle fiber, Ramon is a breath of fresh air.

Of course, that doesn’t mean he just deadlifts and calls it a day. Isolation lifts are just as important for muscle growth as compound lifts, but Queiroz knows where to draw the line.

Operating in an industry where each minute detail is under immense scrutiny, the classic physique bodybuilder includes isolation lifts in his split as well.

Turns out, Ramon Dino doesn’t actually have a published workout routine. As you might know by now, he’s a rather private man, despite his massive online presence. As such, we were only able to find snippets of his workouts from YouTube videos for you. Here’s what his training philosophy comprises of:

As you can see in this video, Ramon Rocha likes going heavy on his compound lifts. He makes use of the chest press machine for added stability in his compound lifts. This stabilizes his range of motion and allows him to go heavier without worrying about his form.

He employs the same principles when benching, where he uses an assisted bench-press machine and the help of a spotter to further stabilize his lifts.

For back, Ramon keeps things classic. He starts off with some barbell rows, before moving on to single-arm lat pulldowns. This way, he engages his latissimus dorsi with maximum intensity.

He also does a few sets of chest-supported machine rows. The added support of the bench combined with the machine’s stability gives him a slight edge in range of motion and form.

For arms, he enjoys spider curls, perhaps for the same reason he likes chest-supported rows. The use of the bench further isolates his arm muscles and allows him to focus the load onto his biceps.

Incline curls are another one of his preferred exercises. He usually performs a brief posing routine before hopping over to the preacher curl machine, where he uses the rope attachment.

The dumbbell shoulder press, machine shoulder press, cable lateral raises, and pec deck flyes are amongst his top exercises on shoulder day.

Leg day starts with a bang with some unilateral leg extensions. Using one leg at a time allows him to focus deeply on his individual legs, one at a time, thus improving his weak points and reducing muscular imbalances that may be caused due to a stronger leg’s bias.

His coach even gives him a (literal) lift to squeeze out a few extra reps. After all, it’s those last few reps where the gains are built.

A post shared by Marcello De Angelis – PRO (@horse.md)

He then does a few squat variations, including one with a set of weights chained to his waist.

Seasonal cardio is a must. Queiroz performs cardio intensely during his on-season routine, while he’s more focused on packing on as much mass as possible during the off-season.

For Ramon, just like any other classic physique competitor, sleep and recovery are just as important as training and nutrition. Sleep regulates your growth hormones, which play a crucial role in muscle mass growth and development.
