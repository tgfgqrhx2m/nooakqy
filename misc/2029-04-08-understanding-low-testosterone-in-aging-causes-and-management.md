Low testosterone (low T) caused by natural aging is known as late-onset hypogonadism (LOH) or andropause.1

This cause of low T is characterized by a decline in Leydig and hypothalamic-pituitary cell function, which can eventually lead to reduced testosterone levels and even hinder sperm production.

It’s essential to differentiate this unique cause of low testosterone, as many health and lifestyle choices can aid a decline in testosterone levels.

Other contributors to low testosterone levels include:

Choices in diet, sleep, and exercise

Medications that may be negatively affecting testosterone levels and production

Other health complications that cause low T as a side effect

For some men, low testosterone levels can be easy to diagnose and manage. In other cases, a doctor may have to do an in-depth investigation to determine the core issue causing low T.

How Low Testosterone Can Affect the Male Body

Healthy testosterone levels play a vital part in the function of several systems within the body. Insufficient levels of testosterone can lead to various other health concerns. That said, it’s important to note that the effects of low T vary on a case-by-case basis.

Without ample levels of testosterone, men could experience some or all of the following health complications:

Reduced muscle mass and physical strength

Complications with bone density

Healthy sexual reproductive functionality

Issues with mood regulation and cognitive function

While some men experience very mild symptoms, others experience more debilitating effects of low T. With appropriate guidance from a licensed and trained healthcare professional, symptoms can be managed effectively.
