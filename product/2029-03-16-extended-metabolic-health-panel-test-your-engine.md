# Extended Metabolic Health Panel: Test Your Engine

Test details

About the Extended Metabolic Health Panel

This add-on layers extra metabolic markers onto your Baseline Blood Panel. Your metabolism is not one thing. It is a system of hormones and signals that determine how your body handles energy: how efficiently you burn fat, how you respond to food, and whether your blood sugar stays stable or swings unpredictably through the day.

Standard metabolic panels check blood sugar and maybe HbA1c. This add-on goes deeper, measuring the hormonal layer that regulates fat storage, appetite, and insulin sensitivity. These markers often shift years before changes appear on standard blood sugar tests.

1 in 3 U.S. adults has metabolic syndrome. Most don't know it, because the early signals don't show up on standard tests.

These markers map how your body regulates blood sugar, produces insulin, and signals hunger and fat storage: the system upstream of where standard blood work looks.

What standard blood sugar tests don't show

HbA1c measures your average blood glucose over the past three months. Fasting glucose gives a snapshot of blood sugar on a single morning. Both are useful core markers, and both measure the end result of a process that may have been developing long before it shows up.

Insulin resistance often begins with the pancreas overproducing insulin to compensate for cells that are becoming less responsive to it. Blood glucose can stay normal during this phase because the pancreas is working harder to keep it there. HbA1c and fasting glucose will not flag this compensated phase. Fasting insulin and C-peptide can.

Leptin and adiponectin add another dimension: how your fat tissue is functioning as a signaling organ. Adipose tissue is not passive storage. It secretes hormones that affect insulin sensitivity, inflammation, and appetite regulation. When these adipokine levels shift, they can reflect metabolic stress that standard glucose tests will not capture.

Frequently asked questions

What is HOMA-IR, and can this panel calculate it?

HOMA-IR (Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance) is a calculated index, not a measured marker, that estimates insulin resistance from fasting insulin × fasting glucose ÷ 405. This add-on supplies the insulin half, and your Baseline Blood Panel supplies the fasting-glucose half, so the index can be calculated when both values are present. It is an index associated with insulin resistance, not a diagnosis. Your care team can help interpret it in context.

How is fructosamine different from HbA1c?

HbA1c reflects your average blood sugar over roughly the past three months, while fructosamine reflects the past two to three weeks. If you have recently changed your diet or activity, fructosamine will surface shifts in glycemic control well before HbA1c would. The two are complementary, not redundant.

What does elevated leptin mean?

Leptin is produced by fat cells in proportion to fat mass, so elevated leptin typically reflects higher fat mass. When leptin stays chronically elevated, the brain can become less responsive to its satiety signal (a state called leptin resistance), which can leave appetite high despite elevated levels. Elevated leptin is associated with insulin resistance, chronic inflammation, and difficulty losing weight.

What does low adiponectin mean?

Adiponectin decreases as fat mass increases. Low adiponectin is associated with reduced insulin sensitivity, increased systemic inflammation, and elevated metabolic and cardiovascular risk. Because it falls rather than rises with worsening metabolic health, it is sometimes described as the "protective" fat hormone, and low adiponectin alongside elevated leptin is a pattern associated with metabolic dysfunction.

How is this different from a standard metabolic panel?

A standard comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP) measures glucose, kidney function, liver enzymes, and electrolytes. It does not include fasting insulin, C-peptide, fructosamine, leptin, or adiponectin. This add-on brings in the hormonal layer of metabolic health (the signaling system that drives how your body manages blood sugar and stores energy), layered on top of your existing blood work, not a replacement for it.

How often should I retest?

Metabolic markers can shift meaningfully with diet, exercise, weight changes, and lifestyle adjustments. Many people retest every 6 to 12 months, or more often when actively working on their metabolic health. Leptin and adiponectin are particularly useful to track alongside body composition changes. Your care team can advise on what cadence makes sense for you.

Biomarkers tested

Adiponectin

Adiponectin is a protein hormone (also called an adipokine) that is mainly secreted by fat cells, but it is also produced in smaller amounts by muscle and the lining of blood vessels.

Method: Laboratory-developed test (LDT) validated under CLIA; not cleared or approved by the FDA. Results are interpreted by clinicians in context and are not a stand-alone diagnosis.

C-Peptide

C-peptide is a short protein fragment released by the pancreas every time it makes insulin.

Fructosamine

Fructosamine is a compound formed when glucose (sugar) in the blood attaches to proteins, mostly albumin.

Method: FDA-cleared clinical laboratory assay performed in CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratories. Used to aid clinician-directed evaluation and monitoring. Not a stand-alone diagnosis.

Insulin

Insulin is a hormone that is produced by the pancreas and regulates the metabolism of glucose, fat and protein.

Leptin

Leptin is a hormone primarily produced by fat cells and released into the bloodstream in amounts that reflect the size of the body’s fat stores.
